Hobby

I’ve always been enthusiastic about art since 5 years ago when I just graduated from my junior high school. I was first influenced by a classmate who was more proficient in drawing at that time. The feeling coming from creating something aesthetically appealing and artistic was genuinely unforgettable, and it continued to be one of the motivations that keeps me improving in this field of art, especially perspective, anatomy, composition and design.

Besides concentrating on using techniques to make my work more fascinating and engaging, I also study how to transfer the specific feelings from my work to the audience by studying various types of aesthetics, including Frutiger Aero, Dreamcore, Vaporwave and so on. It’s an impossible task to choose one as my favorite, but I personally favor Frutiger Aero for its refreshing, clean, hopeful and nostalgic feeling. Its design prevailed from 2004 to 2013 and had been adopted by many products such as the interface of window vista, IDog from Sega Toys and early Nintendo merchandise. It was part of my childhood that can’t be neglected and a future that we were promised. It’s genuinely unfortunate to see Frutiger Aero start declining around 2013 as it was replaced by flat design. Other than this specific aesthetic, those which evoke nostalgia also grow a soft spot in my heart. For instance, early PlayStation game covers enchanted me with great influence that even one of my dream is to make album covers or game covers for composers and game developers. I can’t find a specific style to categorize the game covers but I can identify some of them being Y2K futurism or horror grunge.

I’m also currently learning some art techniques used by a renowned mangaka named Araki Hirohiko for great admiration for him. It’s quite hard to switch from one art style to another. It’s important to resonate with the target artist in terms of their ideologies and philosophies in art because their art styles are basically the summary of their experiences. By inducing Araki’s emphasis and preferences in creating an artwork, I can get to achieve a better understanding of his art style. I’ve been informed that Araki has grown affectionate towards plaster statue and I did feel the same way when I was observing some of his exquisite artworks. The delicate line work can definitely be considered as one of his signature. It usually functions as shades of folds or drapes of cloth and helps fade out from some dark spots. In addition, it also reveals the structure of different objects like muscles, fluctuations of clothing or landforms to emphasize the spatial concept. Some line work is done continuously yet intermittently from a thicker line or alongside a thinner line to indicate a subtle structural change, which can often be seen in cheekbones, hair and clothes. Through observation of his line work, it undeniably shows how much figure sculptures have inspired him to highlight the structure of disparate objects.

I’m currently making a series in Instagram by drawing Jojo characters in different aesthetic to study a wide variety of aesthetics and Araki’s art style at the same time. And some of them have received quite great visibility and recognition on the Internet. I’m also trying to get better at video editing to make my series more engaging.
